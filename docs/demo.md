# Three-minute demo

1. **Library (30 s).** Open the homepage. Every card shows a difficulty badge and hands-on time. Filter *Customer apps* → Gmail and Outlook are **Hard** (provider verification), Slack and HubSpot **Moderate** (admin or marketplace gates). That contrast is the product's point: the hard part of OAuth is usually approval, not code.
2. **A reviewed guide (45 s).** Open **Gmail**. The *At a glance* panel answers the brief directly: difficulty and why, hands-on time vs waiting time, what the agent did, what it can do now, and where you take over. Scroll to *Who needs to say yes?* and expand an evidence chip to show source, conditions and retrieval date.
3. **An unfamiliar app, live (60 s).** Type a provider that is not in the library — e.g. **Attio** (not in the directory; identity inferred and labelled unverified) or **Calendly** (directory entry). Watch the real progress events: docs discovery, pages read, authorize-endpoint check. Open the saved guide: provider-specific registration page, the real authorize/token endpoints, PKCE and token-lifetime quotes, a 1–3 h estimate, and sourced blockers such as `redirect_uri` mismatch.
4. **Directory (15 s).** Scroll to *The directory · 100 OAuth apps*: 21 reviewed guides, 79 one-click live researches.
5. **Getting unstuck (20 s).** On a step, open *I’m stuck here*, describe a redacted redirect mismatch, and show the labelled hypothesis, diagnostic and fallback.
6. **Share (10 s).** Copy the link, export Markdown, download the setup pack. Unknown facts stay as placeholders.

If a docs host is slow or blocks automated reading, show the honest partial result: it names the docs host to open and says why it could not be read. Never present a saved guide as a new live run.
